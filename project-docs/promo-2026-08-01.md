**Different from `social-posts-2026-08-01.md`.** That pack sells *individual fares* and goes stale in
days. This one sells **the product** and stays true for months — no fare numbers, so nothing here
expires. Use this to introduce FareSteal to people who've never heard of it; use the deal pack to
keep them coming back.

⚠️ **Read `## Before you post this` at the bottom first.** There is a live credibility problem on the

## The positioning (everything below is a variation on this)

Every flight site answers *"what does it cost?"*
Nobody answers *"is that a good price?"*

That second question is the entire product. It's also a genuine anxiety — the moment you find a
fare and can't tell whether to book it or wait a week. FareSteal is the only site pointed at it.

**Hero line:** The hard part isn't finding a fare. It's knowing whether to book it.
**Tagline options:** *Know before you book.* · *A verdict, not a sales pitch.* ·
*The only deal site that tells you to wait.*

**The three proof points, in priority order:**
1. **We log it daily.** 14 routes out of Changi, cheapest fare recorded every day since 11 July.
2. **The verdict is against the fare's own history** — not against a made-up "usual price."
3. **We publish the boring verdicts too.** "Wait" is a real answer here. That's the trust engine.

**Never lead with a price.** Every competitor leads with a price. Leading with the *verdict* is the
whole differentiator — and it's the one thing they can't copy without three weeks of tracking.

## Before you post this

**🔴 Fix the homepage first — it currently contradicts the entire promo.**

`apps/web/app/page.tsx` still carries hardcoded mockup numbers from the v2 design that were never
swapped for live data:

- **Hot chips (line ~141):** Tokyo `fr S$312`, Bali `fr S$168`, Seoul `fr S$385`, Bangkok `fr S$142`.
  Real fares on 2026-08-01: Tokyo **$447**, Bali **$183**, Seoul **$310**, Bangkok **$202**.
  Bangkok is understated by 42%.
- **Deal showcase (lines ~195–253):** three hardcoded cards claiming `41% below normal` (Bali),
  `38% below normal` (Tokyo), `29% below normal` (Seoul). **Not one route on the board has hit a
  15%+ discount** — the worker has produced 0 GRAB verdicts against real data (D26 task 4). These
  three badges are fabricated.
- **Trustbar (line ~148):** "60 days of real price tracking." Tracking started 2026-07-11 — that's
  21 days. `/deals` and the route pages honestly say 21; the homepage says 60.

This promo's whole pitch is *"we're the ones who don't make up discount numbers."* Driving traffic to
a homepage advertising a fabricated 41% and a fare 42% below reality is the fastest way to burn the
exact trust the copy is buying. A visitor who clicks Bangkok S$142 and lands on $202 has learned
something about you.

**Fix = swap the hardcoded showcase for live data** (`/deals` already renders it correctly — same
query), and change "60 days" to "tracked daily since 11 July" so it stays true and ages upward
instead of being wrong in both directions. Small Sonnet task.

**Standing rules (same as the deal pack):**
1. Never quote a fare in evergreen promo — that's what the deal pack is for. Promo sells the method.
2. Disclose the affiliate relationship on IG, FB and TikTok. `/disclosure` exists — link it.
3. Any fare number that does appear is **one-way, economy, SGD, ex-SIN.** Say so.
4. Update "since 11 July" to a rolling phrase ("daily for three months") as time passes — or just
   say "every day since 11 July 2026," which never expires.
5. Post the honest verdicts. The "don't book Sydney" post outperforms three deal posts, and it is
   the only reason the "grab it" posts get believed.
